Commercial Kitchen Floor Coating Scope

By Industry Army Marketing (IAM) · Updated September 28, 2026

Drainage, cleaning, slip resistance, transitions and shutdown constraints for food-service floors.

Start with evidence

Record the existing substrate, exposure, damage, access and previous work. Photographs are useful when they are labelled as actual projects or clearly identified as representative.

Define performance and constraints

Identify the required outcome, operating hours, adjacent finishes, ventilation, cure or drying time and documentation obligations before selecting a product.

Request a written scope

The proposal should identify preparation, repairs, materials, application limits, exclusions, protection and the party responsible for inspections or testing.

Verify before closeout

Review appearance, coverage, transitions and required records. Keep product and maintenance information with the property file.

Map the cleaning and drainage routine

Show drains, coves, equipment feet, thresholds and routes used by staff. Describe actual cleaning products and temperatures to the system supplier so compatibility can be reviewed. Ask the designer to resolve drainage and substrate defects before treating a coating as the answer to every floor problem.

Plan a realistic shutdown

Separate removal, preparation, repairs, application and return to service in the programme. Ask which activities can resume at each stage under the selected system’s current instructions. Include equipment movement and reinstatement, then agree how edges and drain interfaces will be inspected before the kitchen reopens.
