Evaluating roof and containment coating projects

By Industry Army Marketing (IAM) · Updated September 28, 2026

Two coating applications where existing condition and exposure determine whether a system is suitable.

For roofs, find the water

Map leaks, seams, drains, penetrations and suspected wet insulation. Coating over trapped moisture or failed components does not correct the underlying assembly.

For containment, define exposure

Identify chemicals, concentrations, temperatures, contact duration and cleaning. Give safety data and process information to the system designer.

Inspect movement and transitions

Cracks, joints, wall-to-floor transitions, terminations and penetrations need compatible details. Movement capability is system-specific.

Request a written scope

A useful proposal identifies preparation, repairs, materials, approximate coverage or thickness, exclusions and site responsibilities.

Safety note

Protective coatings and spray-applied materials can involve hazardous preparation, chemicals, specialized equipment and controlled work zones. This guide supports scope discussions; it is not an application manual.

Keep roof and containment enquiries separate

A roof brief should show drainage, penetrations, existing layers and leak history. A containment brief should identify the intended exposure and operating conditions for review by the system supplier and designer. These are different applications; a product suitable for one should not be carried into the other without evidence.

Resolve concealed conditions

Ask what investigations and substrate repairs precede a firm specification. Mark assumptions and inaccessible areas in the proposal. Include details, terminations, inspections and a written handover so the final scope is more than a coating name and a surface area.
