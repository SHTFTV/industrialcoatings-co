How to scope an industrial floor coating

By Industry Army Marketing (IAM) · Updated September 28, 2026

A practical framework for collecting the information a coating contractor needs before recommending a floor system.

Start with service conditions

Document traffic, point loads, impact, cleaning methods, temperature and every known chemical exposure. “Epoxy floor” is a category, not a complete specification.

Investigate the concrete

Age, moisture, curing compounds, oil, laitance, cracks and old coatings influence preparation. Adhesion and moisture testing may be appropriate before final selection.

Define preparation and details

Mechanical surface preparation, crack treatment, joints, coves, drains, transitions and terminations belong in the scope. These details often control long-term performance.

Plan the work window

Confirm access, ventilation, isolation, cure time and when the area must return to service. Product data and site conditions—not a generic promise—govern timing.

Safety note

Protective coatings and spray-applied materials can involve hazardous preparation, chemicals, specialized equipment and controlled work zones. This guide supports scope discussions; it is not an application manual.

Write an exposure and use schedule

List traffic, loads, cleaning, spills and temperature conditions for supplier review. Distinguish confirmed operating conditions from possibilities. Show joints, drains and transitions on a plan so the scope addresses the floor’s details as well as its main surface.

Define the acceptance process

Ask how preparation and repairs are reviewed before application. Agree appearance expectations and any project-specific checks using the selected system’s documentation. Include the return-to-service instructions in the handover and give the operator a contact for defects or changed exposure conditions.
