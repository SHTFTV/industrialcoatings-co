HiBar Fireproofing Thickness and Quality Control

By Industry Army Marketing (IAM) · Updated September 28, 2026

Why specified thickness, adhesion, substrate and inspection records matter to passive fire protection.

Start with evidence

Record the existing substrate, exposure, damage, access and previous work. Photographs are useful when they are labelled as actual projects or clearly identified as representative.

Define performance and constraints

Identify the required outcome, operating hours, adjacent finishes, ventilation, cure or drying time and documentation obligations before selecting a product.

Request a written scope

The proposal should identify preparation, repairs, materials, application limits, exclusions, protection and the party responsible for inspections or testing.

Verify before closeout

Review appearance, coverage, transitions and required records. Keep product and maintenance information with the property file.

Agree inspection before access disappears

Coordinate the installer, reviewer and other trades around a written inspection sequence. Identify which elements will become difficult to reach and schedule the required checks before concealment. Resolve conflicting drawings and system requirements before application rather than asking the crew to choose between them.

Keep measurements tied to locations

Retain the inspection method, results and correction records in the form required by the project. Use drawing references so a later reviewer can locate the area. An isolated number without context is difficult to interpret; photographs should support the report rather than substitute for the required checks.
